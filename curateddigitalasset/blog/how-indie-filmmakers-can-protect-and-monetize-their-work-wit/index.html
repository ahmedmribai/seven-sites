The 3 Big Problems Filmmakers Face Online Today

Theft of intellectual property is a pervasive issue for indie filmmakers, with many finding their work stolen and sold as NFTs without permission. For instance, a filmmaker may upload their short film to a platform, only to discover that it has been minted as an NFT and is being sold on a marketplace without their consent. The process of getting the stolen work removed can be lengthy and frustrating, involving multiple steps such as filing a DMCA takedown notice and providing proof of ownership. This not only results in lost revenue but also undermines the filmmaker's control over their own creative work.

Overcrowding is another significant challenge, with the sheer volume of content on platforms making it difficult for indie filmmakers to stand out and gain visibility. Algorithms prioritize content that performs well in terms of engagement, making it harder for new or lesser-known creators to break through. For example, a filmmaker may post a trailer for their new film on social media, but it may only reach a fraction of their intended audience due to the algorithmic filtering. This can lead to a situation where high-quality content is effectively invisible, despite the filmmaker's best efforts to promote it.

The rise of AI-generated content has further complicated the landscape, creating unfair competition and devaluing human-made art. AI algorithms can produce high-quality content quickly and cheaply, making it difficult for indie filmmakers to compete. For instance, an AI-generated short film may be able to garner more views and engagement than a human-made film, simply because it is more polished or attention-grabbing. This not only undermines the value of human creativity but also makes it harder for indie filmmakers to monetize their work and build a sustainable career. By understanding these challenges, indie filmmakers can begin to develop strategies to protect and monetize their work in a crowded and rapidly evolving online landscape.
