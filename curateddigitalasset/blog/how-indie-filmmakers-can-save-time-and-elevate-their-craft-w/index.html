Why Generic Templates Don’t Cut It

**Why Generic Templates Don’t Cut It**

Generic templates promise efficiency but often waste more time than they save for indie filmmakers. A one-size-fits-all shot list, for example, might include irrelevant categories for big-budget productions—like "stunt coordinator contacts" or "VFX breakdowns"—while missing crucial indie-specific details such as guerrilla shooting permits or crowdfunding callouts. Filmmakers then spend hours deleting useless sections and rebuilding workflows from scratch, negating the supposed time savings. Worse, these templates lack niche context. A corporate video storyboard won’t account for the pacing, tone, or resource constraints of a micro-budget horror short, forcing creators to retrofit advice that doesn’t apply.

The oversaturation of low-quality, AI-generated templates exacerbates the problem. Marketplaces flood with cookie-cutter assets—like "film production calendars" that ignore festival submission deadlines or "social media kits" with dimensions optimized for brands, not indie trailers. These generic options drown out truly useful tools, mirroring the frustration artists face when their work is lost in a sea of AI-generated content. Meanwhile, stolen or repurposed templates (e.g., a pitch deck copied from a Sundance winner but stripped of its strategic annotations) offer false shortcuts. Without the original creator’s insights—why certain visuals were prioritized, how budget constraints shaped the deck’s flow—filmmakers get a hollow shell that fails under real-world scrutiny.

The result? Indie creators burn limited time sifting through misfit tools, only to realize they’ve traded money for manual labor. A curated pack, by contrast, eliminates this friction by pre-filtering for relevance—like a shot list template that includes a checkbox for "public domain music sources" or a pitch deck with notes on winning over fiscally conservative investors. Specificity is the antidote to wasted effort.
